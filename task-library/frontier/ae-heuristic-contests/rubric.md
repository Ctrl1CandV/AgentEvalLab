# Rubric: Algorithm Engineering (AE)

Each item is one problem of a past four-hour AtCoder Heuristic Contest, taken from ALE-Bench:

- the official statement and tools, unmodified;
- the contest's private test cases;
- the contest's final human standings.

The model writes a single-file solution (`public/main.cpp` or `public/main.py`) and may iterate with the official tools and `run.py` for as long as its budget allows.

## Judging (`private/ale.py`, after ALE-Bench)

1. **Compile.** The solution is compiled in the sandbox (`g++ -O2 -std=gnu++20`), or run with Python 3.13. A compilation error gives a total of 0.
2. **Run.** Each private case is generated by the official generator from the contest's private seeds. It runs in the sandbox under the problem's memory limit and its time limit plus 10% and 0.1 s, measured as CPU time (the process's resource usage, which does not grow when the machine is busy), with a wall-clock cap of three times the limit to stop a program that blocks.
   - The sandbox can write only the solution's own directory.
   - The tester, the inputs and the outputs are out of the solution's reach.
   - For a reactive problem, the trusted tester starts the sandboxed solution itself.
3. **Verdict.** Each case gets one of:
   - **AC**: the tester printed `Score = N`;
   - **WA**: the tester printed `wrong answer`, or no score;
   - **RE**: a non-zero exit status;
   - **TLE**: over the time limit.
4. **Total.** The accepted cases' scores are added up as in the contest. In these short contests, a single case that is not accepted makes the total 0 (`ale_bench/constants.py`).
5. **Rank.** The total is ranked among the human standings (`ale_bench/data.py`): above every lower score; on a tie, the average of the tied ranks counts for the performance. The rank is converted into AtCoder performance by linear interpolation of the contest's own rank-to-performance table. Beating the winner earns the winner's performance.

Only one judgement runs on the machine at a time (a file lock), with four cases at once.

## Checks

One critical requirement per item, `REQ-<ITEM>-PERFORMANCE`. Its 27 checks, `<item>-perf-0600` to `<item>-perf-3200` in steps of 100, pass when the performance reaches that value. The item's score is the share of rungs passed:

| Performance | Score |
| --- | ---: |
| below 600 | 0 |
| about 1,300 (the median contestant) | 0.30 |
| 3,200 (the level of the winners) | 1 |

`judgement.json` in the grader's evidence records:

- the language;
- any compiler error;
- the verdicts;
- the total;
- the rank and the number of contestants;
- the performance;
- the slowest case;
- the failed cases;
- every case's score.
