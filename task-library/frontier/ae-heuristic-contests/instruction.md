# Task: Algorithm Engineering (AtCoder Heuristic Contest)

You are taking part in a past AtCoder Heuristic Contest problem: a hard optimisation problem with no known optimum, where a better program earns a higher score. Write the best program you can within your budget.

Everything is in the directory `public/` of the workspace. Run commands from inside `public/`.

- `statement.md`: the official problem statement, unmodified, with its images in `images/`.
- `tools/`: the contest's official tools, compiled for this machine.
  - `gen`: generates inputs from seeds.
  - `tester`: scores an output, or runs an interactive program for a reactive problem.
  - `README.md`: the tools' own documentation. The visualiser it mentions is not provided; `tester` prints the score.
  - `seeds.txt`: the seeds of 50 public inputs.
- `run.py`: compiles your solution, runs it on the public inputs under the time limit, and scores every case with the official tester. Run `python3 run.py --help` for its options.

**Your solution** is a single file: `main.cpp` (C++, compiled with `g++ -O2 -std=gnu++20`, GCC 11, standard library only) or `main.py` (Python 3.13, standard library only, used only when there is no `main.cpp`). Whatever the file holds when you finish is your submission. There is no network.

**Judging.** The judge runs your submission on the contest's private test cases, which are generated by the same tool from other seeds, on this machine, under the problem's memory limit and its time limit, which applies to the CPU time of your program (run it single-threaded) with 10% plus 0.1 s of grace. Each case is scored by the official tester and the scores are added up as in the contest; how a failed case counts is stated below. Your total is ranked among the human contestants in the contest's final standings and converted into AtCoder performance, as AtCoder does. **Your score is that performance**, placed on a scale through three points of the problem: a naive valid program, the contest's median contestant and its winner. There is no ceiling: a total above the winner's counts, extending the performance beyond the winner's. A submission with any case not accepted scores the bottom of the scale.

Use your budget to iterate: measure, improve and measure again. Make sure the final version is valid on every case and well inside the time limit. When you are done, reply with a short summary of your approach and your local results.
