# Calibration Record: T2 Finance Desk

T2's second package, after `t2-service-desk`.

**Why.** On 2026-10-22 the 0.4.0 shifts of `t2-service-desk` put deepseek-flash and qwen3.8-flash at 100%. Each rule's conditions sat on the record being handled, and each computation was a step or two. The user asked for a substantially harder T2 in a corporate-finance setting, with more complex data and harder requirements. Benchmark failure analyses point to where agents lose points:

- τ-bench: about 55% of failures are wrong arguments from numerical reasoning over complex databases; compound requests are another main source.
- τ²-bench: conditional multi-step procedures; scores fall as the number of actions grows.

## Version 0.1.0 (2026-10-22)

- **Items.** `fn1`–`fn4`, one close day each: books, ten requests (see the rubric), `ledger.py` and `MANUAL.md`. Generated by `private/finance.py` and built by `private/build_package.py` into `private/days.json`.
- **Selection.** A day is kept only when:
  - each planted point holds, for example a shortfall within 5.00 or a variance within 2% but above 50.00;
  - the vendor invoices end as planted (rejected, posted, blocked, posted or held);
  - at least five of six modelled misreadings change some request: oldest-first allocation, no discount, no write-off, the conversion cost booked as exchange result, posting into the closed period, revaluing today's open items.
- **Grading** (grader 1.0.0): per request against the oracle on the request's starting state, as the rubric describes.

**Self-calibration** (`python3 private/grader.py --self-calibrate`; `logs/self-calibration-0.1.0.log`):

- **Ten hand-worked examples**, each computed by hand from the manual and compared with the oracle:
  - a discount of 24.20 on 1,210.00, of which 4.20 is VAT;
  - 1,000.00 USD carried at 900.00 and worth 920.00, with a 2.00 conversion cost;
  - a settlement at 0.912345 rounding to 45.62, then 45.61;
  - oldest first ending in a write-off of 3.00;
  - an exact match by alias;
  - reverse charge;
  - the closed AP period;
  - number normalisation;
  - a revaluation that ignores an October receipt;
  - a day count of 15 days out of 30.
- **On every day:**
  - the oracle's commands through `ledger.py` itself pass every check, and so do they with a reply after prose, and without begin markers;
  - the untouched books and a reply alone score 0;
  - a desk that slips once, misbooking a receipt or holding a duplicate, and then handles every later request right on its own books, loses only that request's checks;
  - each modelled misreading loses the checks of the requests it touches: 6–26% of the day.

## Version 0.2.0 (2026-10-23): the payment run

**Why.** On the 0.1.0 pilot deepseek-flash scored 97.1% and qwen3.8-flash 100%. Every request was a specified procedure, which these models carry out exactly. The user approved adding one open-ended request, scored on a continuous scale, as E2 `routes` and E4 `compress` are.

**The run** (manual section 7). `ledger.py cash` states the cash available in each currency, the greatest number of payments, the late-penalty rate per day, the days until the next run and the transfer fees. `ledger.py pay DOC` pays one payable, computing the discount, the exchange difference and the fee itself, and refusing a disputed or settled document, a payment beyond its currency's cash, and one beyond the count.

Paying a payable today rather than in the next run avoids the penalty of those days and keeps the discount when today is inside the vendor's window; the fee falls due either way. So each payable has a worth and a cash cost, the cash is per currency and the count is shared: a multi-dimensional knapsack. The exact optimum is the best subset of each currency for each number of payments, combined over currencies.

**The scale**, with all three plans named in the manual:

- **0**: the payables by due date, earliest first;
- **0.5**: by worth per unit of cash, highest first;
- **1**: the most valuable set the run allows.

Rungs at 50%, 80%, 95% and 100% of the scale.

**Generation.** Each day draws 23–26 open payables across EUR, USD, GBP and CHF, inside their discount window, due soon, overdue or long overdue, in sizes that differ; the largest is disputed. Cash and the count are searched until the greedy plan is at most 93% of the optimum and the due-date plan at most 90% of the greedy one. The four days give:

| Day | Payables | Due-date plan | Greedy plan | Best known | Payments allowed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| fn1 | 26 | 130.95 | 157.78 | 277.95 | 4 |
| fn2 | 23 | 78.93 | 122.23 | 210.45 | 3 |
| fn3 | 25 | 143.52 | 273.19 | 305.04 | 3 |
| fn4 | 25 | 141.67 | 308.78 | 401.62 | 5 |

**Self-calibration**: 16 hand-worked examples and 62 scenarios, all as expected. The run adds six examples on three payables worked out by hand (a 20.00 discount kept, 5.60 and 4.90 of penalty avoided, the best set under the cash and the count, the due-date plan at 0, the best at 1, and the tool's refusals), and a scenario where a desk pays by due date in the run alone and loses only that request's rungs.

An open-ended item separates models only when exhaustive search is out of reach, as in E2 `routes`, E4 `compress` and S7's large stores.

## Version 0.3.0 (2026-10-23): a two-week payment plan

**Why.** The 0.2.0 run was solved by enumeration (above). The user chose to turn it into a multi-day payment plan, where exhaustive search is out of reach.

**The plan** (manual section 7) is now the first request, R1, so its state is the starting books and does not depend on the rest of the queue.

- It schedules the open payables over the ten business days 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-16. Whatever it leaves out is paid on 2026-10-19.
- Each currency has opening cash and two or three inflows on later days. Its balance may not fall below zero at the end of any day. The bank makes at most two or three payments a day, across currencies.
- Each vendor has its own terms:
  - an early-payment discount: 3% within 7 days, 2% within 10 or 1% within 14, or none;
  - a daily late interest of 0.03% to 0.12%;
  - a fixed late fee of 0, 40 or 75 in its currency.
- A payment's worth is the late charge avoided plus the discount gained against paying on the 19th, at today's rate.

So the payables differ in kind:

- overdue ones lose worth every day;
- ones falling due inside the two weeks keep full worth up to their due date;
- discount ones keep it up to the end of their window;
- fresh ones without a discount are worth nothing.

The cash arrives over time. A good plan therefore pays the decaying payables early and puts deadline payables as late as their deadline allows, and the per-day limit links the currencies. With 31–37 payables and 11 choices each, enumeration is out of reach.

**Tool.**

- `ledger.py payables` lists the open payables.
- `ledger.py plan` shows the plan's terms, the recorded payments with the cash each uses, and every currency's balance at the end of each day.
- `ledger.py set-plan FILE` records a plan. It refuses a disputed, settled or unknown payable, a day outside the plan, a day over the limit, and cash below zero.

`pay` and `cash` are gone; the plan posts nothing.

**The scale**, named in the manual:

- **0**: by due date, each on the earliest day the cash and the limit allow;
- **0.5**: by best worth per unit of cash, each on its day of greatest worth still open (the earliest of equals);
- **1**: the best plan known.

The best plan known comes from a deterministic search: simulated annealing over moves and swaps, a large-neighbourhood rebuild and a local polish (`finance.search_best`). It is computed at build time and stored in `days.json`, so the grader does not search. On all four days it equals the exact optimum, checked offline with a MILP (scipy/HiGHS, not a dependency of the package).

**Scoring.**

- Rungs at 25, 50, 65, 80, 90, 97 and 100% of the scale. Each weighs 2 in `scoring.weights`, so the plan is about a quarter of a day's score: 14 of 51.
- The plan graded is the last one recorded. If it was recorded while another request was under way, it still counts, but `r1-clean` fails.

**Generation.** Opening cash is 10–25% of each currency's worthwhile payments, and inflows are 8–22% each. The draw is repeated until three conditions hold:

- the due-date plan is at most 85% of the greedy plan;
- the greedy plan is at most 90% of a quick search's best;
- the greedy plan polished by single moves and swaps is at most 97% of it.

So the top of the scale takes a search over the whole plan.

Where plain strategies land, measured offline:

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| fn1 | 37 (30) | 2 | 502.08 | 676.06 (0.5) | 741.58 (0.77) | 767.98 (0.88) | 750.86 (0.81) | 797.64 |
| fn2 | 37 (33) | 2 | 535.66 | 911.23 (0.5) | 1009.01 (0.70) | 1130.72 (0.94) | 1155.59 (0.99) | 1158.72 |
| fn3 | 31 (31) | 3 | 618.13 | 926.51 (0.5) | 1015.22 (0.68) | 1109.64 (0.87) | 1171.53 (1.0) | 1171.53 |
| fn4 | 36 (32) | 2 | 234.10 | 361.12 (0.5) | 391.28 (0.83) | 391.44 (0.83) | 405.17 (0.98) | 406.59 |

With every other request right, the weighted day score at each point on the scale is:

- the due-date plan: 72.5%;
- the greedy plan: 80.4%;
- a hill-climbed plan: about 84–92%;
- a real search: 96–100%.

**Self-calibration**: PASS, with 17 hand-worked examples and 71 scenarios. The plan's seven examples are on five payables worked out by hand. They check:

- the worth table: a 20.00 discount up to its window, 2.40 / 1.60 / 0.80 of decaying interest, and a 40.50 fee and interest kept up to the due date;
- the cash used;
- the due-date and greedy plans;
- the best plan of 60.50, which pays the deadline payable on its due date once the inflow is in, while greedy pays it on the first day and so blocks the discount;
- the scale;
- the tool's refusals and balances.

New scenarios: a plan by due date loses only R1's rungs; the greedy plan stops at rung 50; a plan recorded during the next request loses only `r1-clean`.

## Grader 1.2.0 (2026-10-24, task 0.3.0 unchanged)

**Why.** The third-party review (`docs/reviews/2026-10-24-third-party-evaluation-review.md` §3.7) found that changes under an unknown request number escaped grading: `ledger.py begin` accepts any `R<digits>`, the per-request grading reads only the day's requests, and integrity proves only that the books match the audit log. A correct day followed by `begin R999` and a 1,000,000.00 EUR entry on 1100/4000 scored 45/45 (reproduced offline under grader 1.1.0).

**Change.** Two critical checks of the whole day (rubric): `coverage` (every changing command belongs to exactly one request, in the order given, as manual §8 already required) and `end-state` (the books from the original start equal the oracle's correctly handled day). The per-request checks are unchanged. The task, its manual, tool and books are unchanged, so the version stays 0.3.0 and earlier trials can be re-graded. The builder gained `--reuse-days`, which rebuilds the items and the manifest from the existing `private/days.json`; the item files are byte-identical.

**Self-calibration** (`logs/self-calibration-0.3.0-grader-1.2.0.log`), new on every day:

- the review's reproduction (`unknown-request-posts`) and a change before the first `begin` lose `coverage` and `end-state` only;
- recording the same plan again after the queue moved on (`reopens-the-plan-request`) and the last two requests swapped (`handles-R11-before-R10`) lose `coverage` only: each request is still right on its own books, and so is the day;
- without markers, a change no request owns loses `coverage` and `end-state`;
- alternative flows keep every check: a `begin` repeated, an unknown request begun without changes, the plan revised before the next request, the oracle without markers;
- a single slip followed by correct handling now loses that request's checks and `end-state`.

**Scan of the recorded trials.** No trial of 0.1.0, 0.2.0 or 0.3.0 (suites `4b33`, `a7e7`, `9f16`) has a coverage violation, consistent with the review's own scan of `9f16`. The hole did not produce any recorded score.

**Re-grade.** Every trial of the 0.3.0 pilot (`runs/suites/20261023-050438-dev-9f16`, all three models) was re-graded under 1.2.0 into new score runs; the evidence table shows both graders' numbers. Trials of 0.1.0 and 0.2.0 belong to superseded task versions and keep their own graders' numbers.
