# Calibration Record: T2 Finance Desk

T2's second package, after `t2-service-desk`.

**Why.** On 2026-10-22 the 0.4.0 shifts of `t2-service-desk` put deepseek-flash and qwen3.8-flash at 100%. Each rule's conditions sat on the record being handled, and each computation was a step or two. The user asked for a substantially harder T2 in a corporate-finance setting, with more complex data and harder requirements. Benchmark failure analyses point to where agents lose points:

- τ-bench: about 55% of failures are wrong arguments from numerical reasoning over complex databases; compound requests are another main source.
- τ²-bench: conditional multi-step procedures; scores fall as the number of actions grows.

## Version 0.1.0 (2026-10-22)

- **Items.** `fn1`–`fn4`, one close day each: books, ten requests (see the rubric), `ledger.py` and `MANUAL.md`. Generated by `private/finance.py` and built by `private/build_package.py` into `private/days.json`.
- **Selection.** A day is kept only when:
  - each planted point holds, for example a shortfall within 5.00 or a variance within 2% but above 50.00;
  - the vendor invoices end as planted (rejected, posted, blocked, posted or held);
  - at least five of six modelled misreadings change some request: oldest-first allocation, no discount, no write-off, the conversion cost booked as exchange result, posting into the closed period, revaluing today's open items.
- **Grading** (grader 1.0.0): per request against the oracle on the request's starting state, as the rubric describes.

**Self-calibration** (`python3 private/grader.py --self-calibrate`; `logs/self-calibration-0.1.0.log`):

- **Ten hand-worked examples**, each computed by hand from the manual and compared with the oracle:
  - a discount of 24.20 on 1,210.00, of which 4.20 is VAT;
  - 1,000.00 USD carried at 900.00 and worth 920.00, with a 2.00 conversion cost;
  - a settlement at 0.912345 rounding to 45.62, then 45.61;
  - oldest first ending in a write-off of 3.00;
  - an exact match by alias;
  - reverse charge;
  - the closed AP period;
  - number normalisation;
  - a revaluation that ignores an October receipt;
  - a day count of 15 days out of 30.
- **On every day:**
  - the oracle's commands through `ledger.py` itself pass every check, and so do they with a reply after prose, and without begin markers;
  - the untouched books and a reply alone score 0;
  - a desk that slips once, misbooking a receipt or holding a duplicate, and then handles every later request right on its own books, loses only that request's checks;
  - each modelled misreading loses the checks of the requests it touches: 6–26% of the day.

## Version 0.2.0 (2026-10-23): the payment run

**Why.** On the 0.1.0 pilot deepseek-flash scored 97.1% and qwen3.8-flash 100%. Every request was a specified procedure, which these models carry out exactly. The user approved adding one open-ended request, scored on a continuous scale, as E2 `routes` and E4 `compress` are.

**The run** (manual section 7). `ledger.py cash` states the cash available in each currency, the greatest number of payments, the late-penalty rate per day, the days until the next run and the transfer fees. `ledger.py pay DOC` pays one payable, computing the discount, the exchange difference and the fee itself, and refusing a disputed or settled document, a payment beyond its currency's cash, and one beyond the count.

Paying a payable today rather than in the next run avoids the penalty of those days and keeps the discount when today is inside the vendor's window; the fee falls due either way. So each payable has a worth and a cash cost, the cash is per currency and the count is shared: a multi-dimensional knapsack. The exact optimum is the best subset of each currency for each number of payments, combined over currencies.

**The scale**, with all three plans named in the manual:

- **0**: the payables by due date, earliest first;
- **0.5**: by worth per unit of cash, highest first;
- **1**: the most valuable set the run allows.

Rungs at 50%, 80%, 95% and 100% of the scale.

**Generation.** Each day draws 23–26 open payables across EUR, USD, GBP and CHF, inside their discount window, due soon, overdue or long overdue, in sizes that differ; the largest is disputed. Cash and the count are searched until the greedy plan is at most 93% of the optimum and the due-date plan at most 90% of the greedy one. The four days give:

| Day | Payables | Due-date plan | Greedy plan | Best known | Payments allowed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| fn1 | 26 | 130.95 | 157.78 | 277.95 | 4 |
| fn2 | 23 | 78.93 | 122.23 | 210.45 | 3 |
| fn3 | 25 | 143.52 | 273.19 | 305.04 | 3 |
| fn4 | 25 | 141.67 | 308.78 | 401.62 | 5 |

**Self-calibration**: 16 hand-worked examples and 62 scenarios, all as expected. The run adds six examples on three payables worked out by hand (a 20.00 discount kept, 5.60 and 4.90 of penalty avoided, the best set under the cash and the count, the due-date plan at 0, the best at 1, and the tool's refusals), and a scenario where a desk pays by due date in the run alone and loses only that request's rungs.

### Pilot 2026-10-23 (task 0.2.0)

| Model | Score | Median tokens per day | Notes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| deepseek-flash | 100% | 2.64M | the best run on both days |
| qwen3.8-flash | 100% | 3.49M | the best run on both days |
| mimo-v2.6-flash | 34.1% (fn1 only) | 2.13M | stopped by the budget on fn1; fn2 unscored |

mimo's fn2 was unscored because it left a symbolic link in the workspace, which artifact collection refuses (ARTIFACT_LINK_DENIED).

**Why the run did not separate them.** Both models enumerated every allowed set of payments with `itertools.combinations` and paid the best. With about 25 payables and at most 3–5 payments, there are only tens of thousands of sets. An optimum that the oracle can compute by enumeration, a model can compute the same way. An open-ended item separates models only when exhaustive search is out of reach, as in E2 `routes`, E4 `compress` and S7's large stores.
